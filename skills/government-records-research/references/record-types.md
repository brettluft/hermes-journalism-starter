# Record types

Check record types separately. Related records answer different questions.

| Record type | What it can prove | What it does not prove by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Notice or calendar | A body announced a time, place, deadline, or planned event. | That the event occurred or an item was decided. |
| Agenda or order paper | An item was listed for intended consideration. | That it was reached, debated, approved, or implemented. |
| Minutes or official journal | The body's official summary of proceedings and recorded actions. | A verbatim account, unless the body says it is one. |
| Transcript or Hansard | Words recorded or transcribed during proceedings, subject to the publisher's correction policy. | Legal adoption of a proposal or completeness outside the recorded session. |
| Vote | The recorded result, and names only when the artifact records them. | The full debate, motive, implementation, or unrecorded individual positions. |
| Report or attachment | The contents and recommendations of that specific published document. | Adoption, endorsement, or action unless another record says so. |
| Video or captions | Audiovisual evidence of recorded proceedings; captions aid search. | An authoritative legal text. Captions may be automated or inaccurate. |
| Executive order | The signed or officially published directive and its stated terms. | Later implementation, continuing validity, or effects outside its terms. |
| Bill, bylaw, or legal text | The text and status stated in the official artifact. | Enactment, commencement, amendment, or current force unless status records establish it. |

Record the publisher's own artifact name. The labels above are analytical categories, not required local terminology. For every artifact, capture its URL, body, document date, retrieval time, and page, section, or item locator when available.
