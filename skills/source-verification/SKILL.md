# Source verification

Use this workflow when a reporter asks whether a factual claim is true, supported, misleading, or unverified.

## Method

1. Restate the claim narrowly. Split compound claims into separate propositions.
2. Record who made the claim, where it appeared, and when.
3. Find the closest available primary record, such as legislation, a court filing, an official dataset, a company filing, a full speech, or the original study.
4. Find independent sources that can corroborate or contradict the claim. Do not count syndicated copies as independent confirmation.
5. Check dates, jurisdiction, definitions, denominators, units, and whether quoted material is complete enough to preserve its meaning.
6. Look for credible disconfirming evidence before reaching a conclusion.
7. Return an evidence table with the source, publication date, relevant passage or data, source type, and limitations.
8. Label the result as supported, partly supported, contradicted, or unresolved. Explain the label in one paragraph.

## Rules

- Link to every web source and include the retrieval date.
- Identify primary and secondary sources explicitly.
- Never create a quotation from a paraphrase.
- Do not treat search-result snippets as evidence.
- If access is blocked, say so. Do not imply that you read the source.
- Preserve uncertainty. Absence of evidence is not proof that an event did not occur.
- Give the reporter clear steps to reproduce important findings.
