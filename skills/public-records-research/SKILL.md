# Public records research

Use this workflow to locate official records or plan a records-based investigation.

## Method

1. Define the subject, jurisdiction, agency, date range, and record type.
2. List likely custodians and the systems they use, such as meeting portals, court dockets, procurement databases, campaign-finance systems, corporate registries, and legislative archives.
3. Search official sources first. Record the exact query, filters, date, and result URL.
4. Track identifiers such as case numbers, parcel numbers, contract IDs, filing numbers, and agency record-series names.
5. Build a research log that distinguishes records found, records requested, unavailable records, and unanswered questions.
6. Cross-check consequential facts against another record or a knowledgeable human source.
7. If records are unavailable, draft a narrow records request that names the custodian, date range, record type, preferred electronic format, and applicable fee constraints without inventing legal citations.

## Rules

- Do not bypass logins, paywalls, CAPTCHAs, rate limits, or access controls.
- Do not claim a record is absent merely because an online search returned no result.
- Preserve source URLs, access dates, document titles, page numbers, and identifiers.
- Distinguish an official record from an agency statement or press release.
- Minimize collection and publication of personal data unrelated to a legitimate reporting purpose.
- Treat legal deadlines and exemptions as jurisdiction-specific. Ask the reporter to verify them with local counsel or an authoritative guide.
